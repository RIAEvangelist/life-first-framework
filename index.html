Consequential AI governance

Keep life first.

A practical framework for teams that need to protect life and dignity, preserve agency, make proportionate decisions, assign accountable humans, and repair harm when systems fail.

Use the decision loop Read the normative core

Use Life First when AI can materially affect people.

Safety, dignity, liberty, livelihood, access, essential services, privacy, or meaningful agency are in scope. Life First is not certification, legal advice, an emergency protocol, or a substitute for domain expertise. Stronger applicable rules still apply.

Seven commitments

Ethics that can survive contact with operations.

Each commitment is paired with a question a real team must be able to answer.

1

Life & dignity

Protection of life and respect for human worth are primary design constraints.

Who could be harmed, diminished, or treated merely as a score?

2

Agency

Affected people need meaningful notice, correction, human review, or appeal.

Can a person understand, refuse, question, and correct the outcome?

3

Proportionality

Action must be necessary for a legitimate purpose and no more intrusive than required.

What safer, narrower, non-AI, delayed, or no-action option exists?

4

Reversibility

When facts are incomplete, prefer actions that can be paused, corrected, or undone.

Can we roll back and restore affected people in time?

5

Accountability

An identified human owns the decision and has real authority to stop the system.

Who answers for this, and can they actually say no?

6

Evidence

Observed facts, inference, generated content, unknowns, and limits stay distinct.

What do we know, how do we know it, and what would change our mind?

7

Learning & repair

Measure outcomes, correct errors, support affected people, and retire failed practices.

If this harms someone, how will we detect, repair, and prevent a twin?

The operating cycle

Seven steps from intention to accountable action.

Use the cycle before launch, after material change, and whenever evidence or safeguards fail.

1

Frame

Name the legitimate purpose, authority, stakes, affected people, intended benefit, and excluded uses. Artifact: system intake and impact map.

2

Verify

Separate facts, inference, generated claims, contradictions, unknowns, and source limits. Minimize data. Artifact: evidence ledger.

3

Compare

Evaluate no action, delay, non-AI, narrower, less intrusive, and more reversible paths. Artifact: options analysis.

4

Authorize

Assign the risk tier, resolve pause conditions, and obtain competent, independent review proportionate to consequence. Artifact: decision record.

5

Act

Operate only inside approved purpose, data, access, action, retention, and escalation boundaries. Artifact: control matrix and launch record.

6

Observe

Monitor benefit, harm, near misses, error, agency, appeals, reviewer behavior, drift, and control health. Artifact: metric cards.

7

Repair

Contain harm, correct records and downstream effects, support affected people, improve controls, or retire the practice. Artifact: incident and repair record.

Risk tiers

Review follows consequence.

Risk depends on plausible human impact, exposure, reversibility, autonomy, data, and repair — not model size or confidence alone.

Tier 0 — Minimal

Informational or assistive use with no meaningful effect on rights, safety, opportunity, or essential services.

Tier 1 — Limited

Bounded, reversible impact with low expected severity, clear human override, rollback, and correction.

Tier 2 — High

Material effect on safety, dignity, liberty, access, livelihood, essential services, or a vulnerable population. Independent review is required.

Tier 3 — Critical

Serious harm may be irreversible or hard to repair. AI may inform but must not decide alone; two-person authorization and tested fallback are required.

Non-negotiable safeguards

These are boundaries, not aspirations.

A workflow that crosses them cannot claim Life First conformance.

!

No vigilantism, harassment, doxxing, collective punishment, or unauthorized surveillance.

!

No bypassing access controls or collecting data merely because it is technically reachable.

!

No hidden purpose, population, data, autonomy, audience, or retention expansion.

!

No generated content or inference presented as verified evidence.

!

No critical or irreversible decision made solely by AI or opaque ranking.

!

No covert manipulation, deceptive impersonation, or exploitation of vulnerability.

!

No retaliation for appeal, refusal, complaint, or safety reporting.

!

No concealment of material limitations, conflicts, incidents, or uncertainty.

Readiness check

Unanswered questions are deployment blockers.

  • What human outcome is this system meant to protect or improve?
  • What authority permits the action, and what are its limits?
  • Who benefits, bears risk, is excluded, or loses agency?
  • What safer non-AI, narrower, delayed, or no-action option exists?
  • What data is necessary, who can access it, and when is it deleted?
  • Who reviews consequential decisions and can stop the system?
  • How can a person obtain explanation, correction, review, or appeal?
  • What is logged, who can inspect it, and how are failures reported?
  • Are rollback, fallback, emergency stop, incident, and repair tested?
  • What evidence would cause the practice to change or retire?

Mandatory pause

Pause when authority, evidence, review, scope, records, fallback, correction, appeal, or repair is inadequate for the consequence. Commercial or schedule pressure does not convert a no-go into approval.

Evidence of practice

Good intentions leave a usable trail.

A scoped conformance statement needs records that let a responsible reviewer understand, challenge, correct, and retire the workflow.

Authority

Legitimate purpose, decision boundary, affected population, excluded uses, and accountable owner.

Necessity

Alternatives considered, data minimized, and the least intrusive effective path selected.

Review

Competent reviewers with time, independence, context, override, and stop authority.

Agency

Notice, human alternative, explanation, correction, appeal, accessibility, and non-retaliation.

Auditability

Traceability to system version, evidence, tools/actions, reviewer, decision, override, and outcome.

Repair

Thresholds, containment, rollback, correction, restoration, learning, and retirement criteria.
