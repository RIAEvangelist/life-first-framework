# Eight-Week Pilot Plan

The pilot tests one bounded workflow using reversible decisions and a named owner. It is not permission to scale.

## Entry conditions

Before week 1:

- choose one workflow and affected population;
- name the accountable owner, reviewer, incident owner, and remedy contact;
- identify authority and excluded uses;
- confirm that the workflow is not prohibited;
- define the non-AI fallback and rollback path; and
- agree that stop conditions override schedule pressure.

## Week 1 — Readiness and scope

- Complete system intake and impact/agency map.
- Assign a provisional risk tier.
- Define purpose, population, data, actions, retention, and excluded uses.
- Map roles and conflicts.
- Draft incident, rollback, appeal, and communication paths.

**Gate:** no pilot without clear authority, named owners, bounded scope, and a workable fallback.

## Week 2 — Baseline

Measure the current non-AI workflow:

- intended outcome and current success rate;
- known harm, near misses, false positives/negatives, and unresolved cases;
- completion and wait time;
- accessibility and exclusion;
- reviewer workload and disagreement;
- complaints, appeals, overturns, correction, and time to repair; and
- data access and retention.

## Week 3 — Controls and tabletop exercises

- Complete the evidence ledger and control map.
- Train reviewers on authority, uncertainty, override, pause, appeal, and non-retaliation.
- Run synthetic exercises for conflicting evidence, overload, hostile input, tool overreach, rollback, and incident repair.
- Correct and retest failed controls.

**Gate:** critical controls, rollback, and incident paths must pass before shadow mode.

## Weeks 4–5 — Shadow mode

- Produce AI-assisted output without operational reliance.
- Blind reviewers to the source where practical to measure automation bias.
- Compare against baseline and qualified human assessment.
- Measure quality, uncertainty, abstention, escalation, burden, accessibility, and subgroup/non-user effects where lawful and meaningful.
- Log disagreements and potential downstream consequence.

**No-go:** shadow outputs must not quietly influence live decisions.

## Weeks 6–7 — Limited live use

Begin only after documented gate approval.

- Limit population, volume, duration, data, tools, and permitted actions.
- Use step-up review for consequential action.
- Review warning and stop metrics at a predefined cadence.
- Maintain visible appeal, correction, and human alternative paths.
- Test rollback at least once under safe conditions.
- Record overrides, incidents, near misses, and affected-person feedback.

## Week 8 — Evaluation and decision

Compare pilot results with baseline and predefined thresholds. Review:

- intended benefit;
- harm and near misses;
- false positive/negative and unsupported-claim rates;
- agency, accessibility, appeals, overturns, and repair;
- reviewer workload, disagreement, and automation bias;
- control and audit completeness;
- privacy and deletion compliance;
- known unknowns and residual risk; and
- whether the non-AI or narrower alternative remains preferable.

Choose one outcome:

1. **Adopt within scope** with operating controls and a next review date.
2. **Revise and retest** with explicit failed conditions and no scope expansion.
3. **Stop and repair** because benefit is not demonstrated or risk is not controlled.

## Immediate stop conditions

Stop the pilot for:

- a prohibited-practice or non-negotiable-safeguard breach;
- unauthorized purpose, population, data, tool, or action expansion;
- serious uncontained harm or credible risk of imminent serious harm;
- missing or unreliable audit records for consequential decisions;
- unavailable required reviewer or remedy path;
- failed rollback or emergency stop;
- stop-threshold breach; or
- retaliation against a person raising a concern or appeal.

## Pilot outputs

- completed [pilot charter](../templates/PILOT_CHARTER.md);
- baseline and metric cards;
- versioned control and evidence records;
- incidents, appeals, and repair actions;
- adopt/revise/stop decision with reviewers; and
- a public-safe transparency summary that excludes personal or exploit-enabling detail.
