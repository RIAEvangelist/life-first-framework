# Life First Charter

## Mission

Life First exists to help people design and operate AI-assisted systems that protect life and dignity, preserve human agency, act proportionately, remain accountable to identifiable humans, and learn openly enough to repair harm.

The framework turns ethical intent into requirements, records, decision rights, stop conditions, and evidence.

## Scope

Life First applies when an AI system informs, recommends, prioritizes, communicates, or acts in a workflow that can materially affect:

- physical or psychological safety;
- dignity, privacy, reputation, or freedom from manipulation;
- liberty, legal position, or access to remedy;
- employment, education, housing, credit, insurance, or essential services;
- access to public benefits or community resources;
- a vulnerable, dependent, or historically burdened population; or
- the ability of a person to understand, question, refuse, or correct a consequential outcome.

The unit of review is a **specific system in a specific workflow for a specific population and purpose**. A model, vendor, or organization is not Life First merely because one workflow has been reviewed.

## Commitments

1. Protect life and dignity as primary constraints.
2. Preserve informed agency and contestability.
3. Use legitimate authority and a clearly bounded purpose.
4. Seek reliable evidence and disclose uncertainty.
5. Prefer necessary, proportionate, and reversible action.
6. Assign named, competent, empowered human accountability.
7. Minimize data, access, retention, and disclosure.
8. Monitor outcomes, including burdens on non-users and affected groups.
9. Stop, correct, repair, and retire when controls fail.
10. Make public claims no broader than the retained evidence.

## Non-goals

Life First does not:

- certify an organization, vendor, model, or person;
- provide legal, medical, emergency, or other professional advice;
- replace applicable law, safety standards, labor agreements, or professional duties;
- authorize surveillance, vigilantism, coercion, or collection outside legitimate authority;
- promise that harm can be eliminated;
- turn ethics into one composite score; or
- treat the presence of a human reviewer as sufficient without competence, time, information, and authority.

## Prohibited practices

The following practices cannot claim Life First conformance:

- autonomous action intended to unlawfully kill, injure, restrain, or deprive a person of an essential service;
- a critical or irreversible decision made solely from model output, opaque ranking, or uncorroborated inference;
- covert manipulation, deceptive impersonation, vulnerability targeting, or emotional exploitation that suppresses informed choice;
- unauthorized surveillance, doxxing, stalking, discriminatory profiling, or unnecessary inference of sensitive traits;
- presenting generated content or inference as verified evidence;
- retaliation against a person who appeals, reports harm, refuses consent, or raises a safety concern;
- controls designed to conceal incidents, defeat oversight, bypass an authorized stop, or make accountability impossible; or
- purpose, population, data, autonomy, or retention expansion without a new review.

## Stable definitions

- **Dignity:** treatment of a person as an end with inherent worth, not merely as a data source, risk score, target, or optimization constraint.
- **Agency:** a person's meaningful ability to understand, choose, refuse, question, correct, or appeal within the limits of the context.
- **Consequential decision:** an outcome with a plausible material effect on safety, rights, dignity, liberty, opportunity, access, essential services, or livelihood.
- **Accountable human:** an identified person with the competence, information, authority, time, and obligation to own, challenge, stop, and answer for a decision.
- **Reversibility:** the practical ability to pause or undo an action and restore affected people without creating disproportionate additional harm.
- **Repair:** action that contains harm, corrects records and downstream effects, supports affected people, restores access or opportunity where possible, and prevents recurrence.
- **Affected person:** anyone who bears material benefit, burden, risk, exclusion, surveillance, or loss of agency, whether or not they are the direct user.

Additional terms are defined in the [glossary](GLOSSARY.md).

## Framework stewardship

The public framework is maintained through traceable proposals, provenance review, safeguard-impact review, and versioned releases. Repository governance and operational accountability are described separately in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).

## Review

This charter should be reviewed at least annually and whenever the framework's mission, risk tiers, prohibited practices, conformance model, or governance materially changes.
