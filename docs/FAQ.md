# Frequently Asked Questions

## Is Life First a certification?

No. It is an open working framework. A scoped self-attestation may describe one system and workflow, but it must not imply independent certification or organization-wide approval.

## Does Life First mean an AI should always act when harm is possible?

No. Protective intent does not create authority. Action must be lawful, evidence-based, necessary, proportionate, and inside the approved scope. Delay, abstention, human escalation, or no action may be the safer path.

## Is a human in the loop enough?

No. Meaningful review requires competence, adequate information, enough time, independence, workload capacity, and authority to disagree, override, or stop.

## Can a high model confidence justify a critical decision?

No. Model confidence is not authority, evidence quality, or consequence analysis. Tier 3 decisions require corroborated evidence and accountable human authorization; AI may inform but must not decide alone.

## Does public data mean unrestricted use is acceptable?

No. Technical accessibility does not establish authority, necessity, fairness, or a reuse license. Purpose, privacy, access controls, sensitivity, and downstream effect still matter.

## Can teams calculate one Life First score?

They should not. A single score hides tradeoffs and invites gaming. Report protection, agency, quality, governance, and learning dimensions separately with counter-metrics.

## What if urgency makes the normal process too slow?

Use a bounded emergency path with explicit authority, owner, evidence minimum, scope, permitted action, monitoring, expiration, and after-action review. Urgency does not justify a prohibited practice or permanent exception.

## What if a safer non-AI option exists?

Document and compare it. AI should not be used merely because it is available. If the non-AI option is safer, effective, and proportionate, it may be the correct choice.

## What belongs in a public transparency report?

Purpose, version, accountable organization, intended and excluded uses, AI's role, broad data practices, limitations, oversight, appeal route, evaluation date, outcomes, and safe incident/correction summaries. Exclude personal, protected, or exploit-enabling details.

## When should a workflow be retired?

When intended benefit is not demonstrated, serious risk cannot be controlled, required authority ends, correction or appeal is ineffective, a critical dependency becomes unreliable, or the safer alternative is to stop.

## May external frameworks be incorporated?

Yes, when provenance and compatible reuse terms are verified. When a public source lacks clear reuse terms, link and attribute it rather than copying it.

## Is Life First a QuietWire or Civic AI Canon subproject?

No. Life First is independently stewarded and keeps its own repository and public site. Under the agreed hybrid collaboration model, selected public artifacts may be deliberately proposed or contributed to Civic AI Canon with provenance links in both directions. No contribution, acceptance, reciprocal link, endorsement, sponsorship, or affiliation is claimed until a specific public record establishes it.

## Are the casebook examples operating instructions?

No. They are fictional governance exercises using synthetic details. They do not replace religious leadership, clinical judgment, law, intelligence doctrine, engineering standards, emergency procedures, or mission rules. A real team must use current domain requirements and qualified authorities, then document how the Life First cycle fits around them.
