# Life First Casebook

This casebook shows how the Life First cycle changes across very different high-consequence settings. Every scenario is **fictional**, uses synthetic details, and is designed for tabletop learning rather than live operations.

The examples are not religious rulings, clinical guidance, legal advice, intelligence doctrine, industrial procedures, emergency protocols, or mission rules. Qualified authorities and the current rules that govern the real setting remain controlling.

## How to use an example

1. Read the scenario and identify what has been intentionally excluded.
2. Re-run the seven-step cycle using the actual authority, environment, affected people, and governing procedures for your setting.
3. Create the named records from the [toolkit](../templates/README.md); do not copy fictional approvals or thresholds into production.
4. Exercise the workflow in a tabletop or shadow mode before any operational reliance.
5. Test loss of communications, conflicting evidence, unavailable reviewers, failed fallback, and pressure to continue.
6. Record who can stop the workflow, how people can dissent, and what repair is possible after a mistake.

## Casebook-wide boundaries

- No example authorizes AI to decide theology, diagnose or treat a person, authorize force, identify a target, override engineered protection, bypass an interlock, command a vehicle, or approve a mission phase.
- A model output is never treated as verified evidence merely because it is confident, repeated, or operationally convenient.
- Critical decisions require corroborated evidence, named human authority, independent review, a tested fallback, and a usable stop path.
- Remote or time-delayed conditions increase the need for bounded local authority; they do not expand AI authority.
- Transparency must not disclose personal, clinical, classified, security-sensitive, or exploit-enabling information.
- Each example preserves a human or non-AI path and defines conditions under which the AI-assisted workflow is unavailable.
