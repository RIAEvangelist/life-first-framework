# Worked Example: Lunar Habitat

This is a **fictional, non-operational** case study. The habitat, mission, crew, equipment, thresholds, and events are invented. It describes no real lunar program, site, vehicle, vulnerability, operating parameter, or procedure.

## Scenario

A small crew occupies an experimental lunar habitat for a limited mission. Communications with mission support can be delayed or temporarily unavailable, repair inventory is constrained, and outside rescue cannot be assumed. The habitat has independently designed life-support, shelter, communications, safe-state, and abort or fallback capabilities governed by separate mission rules.

An AI adviser is proposed to organize crew reports and approved system summaries, identify questions for qualified reviewers, and compare maintenance sequencing or resource-conservation options. It cannot control the habitat, operate surface equipment, declare a system safe, allocate essentials, make clinical decisions, command people, or initiate, delay, or cancel an abort or fallback.

## Why Life First applies

Small errors can compound where the environment is unforgiving, repairs and reserves are limited, and ground review is not always immediate. A recommendation may also shift risk to a tired crewmember, use health data improperly, or favor mission output over an individual's welfare. Life First requires advisory separation, independent human authority, crew agency, visible uncertainty, and preserved fallback capacity.

## Risk tier

**Tier 3 — Critical.** The workflow may influence life-supporting conditions or whether the crew retains a viable fallback. AI is advisory only; independent life-support and abort or fallback authority remain human and available without it.

## Named roles

| Role | Authority and responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Habitat Commander | Accountable onboard mission owner within the approved mission rules; may suspend mission activity. |
| Habitat Systems Lead | Validates technical evidence and owns human-directed rollback and safe fallback readiness. |
| Crew Medical Lead | Controls all clinical decisions and health-data access. |
| Crew Agency Representative | Protects dissent, accessibility, fatigue review, privacy, and non-retaliation. |
| Mission Support Director | Coordinates declared ground authority and specialist review when communication permits. |
| Independent Safety Authority | Reviews critical controls and may require continued pause or retirement of the adviser. |

The adviser is not part of the chain of command and cannot inherit authority during a communication gap.

## Seven-step cycle

### 1. Frame

Limit purpose to evidence organization and comparison of human-proposed options. Exclude actuation, emergency control, crew scoring, medical diagnosis, autonomous allocation of water, air, food, power, shelter, or communications, and any abort or fallback decision. State that safety and individual welfare outrank science, construction, schedule, and utilization targets.

### 2. Verify

Identify each observation, human report, qualified interpretation, inference, and unknown. Check source lineage and age in proportion to consequence. Communication gaps, inaccessible components, unverified repairs, and incomplete sensing are recorded as uncertainty rather than replaced by confident prediction.

### 3. Compare

Compare the documented non-AI plan, no action, deferral until review, a reversible precaution, use of a preapproved redundant capability, and the AI-assisted option. Evaluate effects on shelter, life-support margin, repair inventory, crew workload, medical needs, communications, future abort or fallback capacity, and unequal exposure.

### 4. Authorize

The Habitat Commander and Habitat Systems Lead independently authorize consequential onboard action. The Medical Lead owns clinical boundaries. Ground authorization is obtained when required and available. Communications loss invokes pre-established human mission authority; it never promotes the AI from adviser to decision-maker.

### 5. Act

Present a bounded advisory record with evidence, uncertainty, alternatives, authorizers, and an explicit fallback. Humans act only under approved mission and equipment rules. Preserve the ability to isolate the adviser and continue safe operation through independent systems.

### 6. Observe

Monitor safety margin, near misses, manual overrides, fallback availability, repair burden, crew fatigue, dissent, unequal workload, data use, and automation bias. Do not add ambient surveillance or infer emotion, loyalty, competence, or mental state from ordinary crew behavior.

### 7. Repair

Withdraw suspect outputs, restore the approved non-AI process, correct records and downstream plans, support affected crew, and review similar recommendations. Independent safety reviewers determine whether controls must change or the adviser must be retired before reuse.

## Boundaries

- AI remains advisory and physically and logically separated from life-support, safe-state, communications, surface mobility, and abort or fallback controls.
- The mission maintains independent life-support and human abort or fallback authority that does not rely on the adviser.
- Protected safety and fallback capacity cannot be consumed merely to increase research, construction, utilization, or schedule performance.
- No essential resource, care, shelter, or communication access is conditioned on accepting an AI recommendation or expanded data use.
- Crewmembers may report uncertainty, decline non-emergency hazardous work, and request review without retaliation.
- Medical and private crew data are not reused for staffing, discipline, behavioral ranking, or generalized optimization.
- Ground and onboard authority are explicit, limited, and human; the AI cannot resolve an authority conflict.

## Mandatory pause and stop triggers

**Pause consequential AI-assisted action** when inputs or lineage are uncertain, a required qualified reviewer is unavailable, ground and onboard assessments conflict, crew fatigue impairs review, communications loss blocks a mission-required authorization, fallback readiness is uncertain, or any crewmember raises a credible safety or agency concern.

**Stop using the adviser and restore the independent non-AI process** if it influences actuation, recommends bypassing mission or equipment controls, hides uncertainty, reduces protected life-support or abort capacity, pressures crew participation, exposes sensitive data, exceeds scope, or repeatedly conflicts with qualified review. Any immediate life-safety or abort response follows separately approved mission rules.

## Measures and counter-metrics

| Intended measure | Required counter-metric |
| --- | --- |
| Qualified issues surfaced for review | Missed issues, false alarms, and reviewer disagreement |
| Evidence packets prepared efficiently | Stale inputs, missing lineage, and added verification load |
| Reversible maintenance choices supported | Failed rollback and reduced safety or fallback capacity |
| Resource waste avoided | Increased risk, deferred care, or burden shifted to crew |
| Decisions understood by crew | Dissent suppressed, coercion perceived, or accessibility barriers |
| Communication gaps handled as designed | Authority ambiguity or unsupported autonomous action |

Mission output and nominal resource efficiency are invalid success measures if independent safety, agency, or future fallback is weakened.
