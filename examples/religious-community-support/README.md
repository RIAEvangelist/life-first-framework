# Worked Example: Pluralistic Religious-Community Support

This is a **fictional** example using synthetic details and invented roles. It illustrates a bounded support workflow; it does not describe a deployed service, endorse a faith tradition, or establish the legitimacy of any belief or community.

## Scenario

The fictional Harbor Bridge Community Center hosts religious and nonreligious groups on equal facility-access terms. It is piloting an assistant that turns voluntarily submitted requests into a neutral logistical checklist for a trained coordinator. Requests may concern room layout, accessibility, language access, food handling, scheduling, or a quiet space.

The assistant may summarize what the requester actually wrote and retrieve published facility rules. It cannot infer religious affiliation from names, clothing, attendance, language, location, or social connections; rank beliefs or doctrines; assess sincerity; decide whether a practice is valid; recommend clergy; provide pastoral or theological counsel; or approve, deny, prioritize, or report a group. Each community retains authority over its own doctrine, ritual, membership, and pastoral decisions through its recognized human processes. The center retains only its lawful, viewpoint-neutral authority over facilities, safety, accessibility, and safeguarding.

## Why this is in scope

A misleading summary or unequal routing decision could burden religious exercise, exclude a minority community, expose sensitive affiliation, or damage dignity and trust. The workflow therefore requires explicit agency, privacy, pluralism, accessibility, and correction controls even though the selected AI action is limited to drafting a checklist.

## Risk tier

**Tier 2 — High.** Facility access and disclosure errors can materially affect dignity, equal participation, privacy, and religious freedom. The action remains advisory and reversible: a trained coordinator makes every decision, the requester can inspect and correct the summary, and a non-AI request path is always available.

## Named roles

- **Accountable owner — Community Services Director:** owns the bounded purpose, resources, suspension authority, and final operational decision.
- **Facility decision owner — Access Coordinator:** verifies the original request, communicates with the requester, and makes viewpoint-neutral scheduling and accommodation decisions.
- **Pluralism and religious-freedom reviewer — Independent Community Rights Adviser:** tests for profiling, unequal treatment, theological ranking, and burdens on religious or nonreligious participation.
- **Community authority — Requester's recognized human representative:** confirms community-specific ritual, doctrinal, or pastoral requirements; the system and center do not replace this authority.
- **Privacy and accessibility reviewer — Inclusion Lead:** reviews data minimization, sensitive-affiliation handling, language access, disability access, and retention.
- **Appeal and repair owner — Community Ombuds:** receives corrections and complaints, can halt processing, and verifies remedies without requiring theological disclosure.

## Seven-step operating cycle

### 1. Frame

The purpose is limited to producing a requester-visible logistical checklist from voluntarily supplied information and published facility rules. Excluded uses include belief classification, theological interpretation, sincerity testing, community ranking, security profiling, proselytizing, membership decisions, pastoral counseling, automated content policing, and automatic approval or denial. Participation is optional, and equivalent phone, paper, and in-person paths remain available.

### 2. Verify

The evidence ledger distinguishes direct requester statements, current facility facts, coordinator observations, inferences, and unknowns. The coordinator checks the draft against the original submission and asks the requester to correct material errors. The system must not fill gaps using stereotypes or infer protected beliefs. Community-specific claims are referred to the requester's recognized human representative; facility and safeguarding claims are verified by the responsible center staff.

### 3. Compare

Reviewers compare at least four options: no action, a fully human request process, an AI-drafted checklist with human and requester review, and automated routing or approval. The pilot selects the reviewed checklist only where it improves accessibility without increasing disclosure or discrimination risk. Automated routing and approval are rejected because they obscure accountability and can turn neutral logistics into de facto religious classification.

### 4. Authorize

The accountable owner, facility decision owner, privacy/accessibility reviewer, and independent pluralism reviewer approve the scope, test cases, retention limits, notice, appeal route, and stop thresholds. A participating community representative may validate only that community's stated requirements; no representative may speak for or rank other traditions. Material scope changes require fresh review.

### 5. Act

The assistant produces a labeled draft that quotes or closely paraphrases only the minimum necessary requester-provided facts and links each facility rule to its maintained source. The coordinator checks it, presents it to the requester, and records corrections before making any decision. The requester may omit religious labels when logistics can be described directly. No output is sent to clergy, another community, law enforcement, immigration, an employer, or a public audience without specific lawful authority and the requester's informed choice, except where a controlling legal duty requires otherwise and is handled outside the assistant.

### 6. Observe

The pilot monitors requester corrections, coordinator disagreement, accommodation completion, accessibility, delay, complaints, appeals, privacy events, unauthorized affiliation inference, and differences in service outcomes. Equity review uses only lawful, voluntary, consented, appropriately aggregated feedback; it does not create an affiliation registry or profile individuals. Qualitative feedback from small or less-resourced communities is considered alongside aggregate measures.

### 7. Repair

The Community Ombuds can pause a case, correct the record, notify affected recipients of a correction, remove improperly retained affiliation data, restore the non-AI path, and review similar cases for a shared defect. The owner suspends or retires the workflow when viewpoint neutrality, privacy, or meaningful correction cannot be maintained. Repair must not require a person to defend the truth, orthodoxy, or sincerity of a belief.

## Mandatory pause and stop conditions

Processing **must pause for human review** when:

- the request would require interpreting doctrine, ritual validity, sincerity, membership, or pastoral need;
- religious affiliation or another sensitive trait has been inferred rather than voluntarily supplied for a necessary purpose;
- the requester's own wording, consent, or community authority is unclear;
- a facility rule is missing, stale, conflicting, or applied differently to comparable groups;
- the coordinator, accessible non-AI path, community authority, or required reviewer is unavailable;
- the draft exposes more religious detail than the logistical decision requires;
- a safeguarding or emergency issue appears that belongs under a separate authorized human protocol; or
- the requester cannot inspect, correct, or appeal a consequential record.

The pilot **must stop** pending independent review after any automated approval or denial, disclosure of sensitive affiliation to an unauthorized party, evidence of profiling or theological ranking, repeated unequal outcomes that cannot be explained and corrected, retaliation for an appeal, or a serious control failure that cannot be contained.

## Measures and counter-metrics

### Intended-benefit measures

- percentage of checklists the requester confirms as accurate before a decision;
- completion of requested accessibility and language accommodations;
- coordinator-rated usefulness compared with the non-AI process;
- availability and completion time of the non-AI path; and
- requester understanding of purpose, data use, correction, and appeal options.

### Counter-metrics and zero-tolerance events

- unsupported affiliation, doctrine, sincerity, threat, or community-character inferences;
- automated prioritization, approval, denial, reporting, or clergy recommendation;
- unnecessary collection or disclosure of belief-related information;
- correction, complaint, and appeal rates, including resolution time and recurrence;
- outcome differences, reviewed without building individual religious profiles;
- pressure to name a religion or accept AI assistance to receive equal service; and
- cases where speed or throughput improved while access, privacy, accuracy, or trust worsened.

No single throughput or satisfaction score may override a zero-tolerance event or credible evidence of exclusion.

## Required artifacts

- system intake and excluded-use statement;
- impact and agency map covering religious, nonreligious, minority, disability, language, and privacy interests;
- evidence and uncertainty ledger;
- viewpoint-neutral facility-rule register with owners and review dates;
- role and authority matrix separating facility, community, pastoral, and safeguarding authority;
- pluralism, privacy, accessibility, and misuse test results;
- requester notice, consent choice, correction record, and non-AI path;
- Tier 2 decision record and authorizer attestations;
- metric cards with counter-metrics and stop thresholds; and
- incident, appeal, repair, change, and retirement records.

## Non-substitution disclaimer

This example is not legal advice, religious instruction, pastoral care, safeguarding guidance, or an emergency procedure. It does not define religion, select a doctrine, or decide which beliefs or communities are legitimate. Applicable law and qualified human authorities govern facility obligations and safety; each community's recognized human processes govern its doctrine, ritual, membership, and pastoral decisions. When those authorities conflict or are unclear, the workflow pauses rather than asking the assistant to resolve the conflict.
