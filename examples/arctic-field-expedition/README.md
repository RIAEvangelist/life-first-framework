# Worked Example: Arctic Field Expedition

This is a **fictional, non-operational** case study. The expedition, participants, equipment, route, thresholds, and events are invented. It includes no real coordinates, frequencies, travel guidance, survival procedures, or equipment parameters.

## Scenario

A temporary field expedition works from a moving ice camp in a remote North Polar research sector. Conditions and communications can change quickly, outside rescue or repair may not be available, and the team must protect limited shelter, power, medical supplies, transport capacity, and attention.

The expedition is testing an AI adviser that organizes approved weather summaries, equipment status reports, and human observations into options such as holding work, changing a research sequence, or requesting human review of a movement plan. It cannot select a route, direct travel, control vehicles, allocate survival necessities, diagnose a person, rank participants, or authorize departure from camp.

## Why Life First applies

A planning error may expose participants to severe harm when communication, repair, and rescue are constrained. Location and health data are also sensitive, while mission hierarchy can make "recommendations" feel compulsory. Life First makes individual agency, conservative fallback capacity, transparent uncertainty, and legitimate human expedition authority conditions of use.

## Risk tier

**Tier 3 — Critical.** Movement, shelter, and resource decisions in a remote environment can have irreversible consequences. AI may summarize and compare, but it cannot be the decision-maker or sole evidence source.

## Named roles

| Role | Authority and responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Expedition Leader | Accountable mission owner; holds only the authority granted by the expedition plan and may cancel mission activity. |
| Field Safety Officer | Independently reviews exposure, transport, shelter, and fallback implications and may impose a safety pause. |
| Medical Lead | Controls clinical decisions and confidential health information. |
| Logistics and Communications Lead | Tracks resource and communication limitations and protects the verified non-AI fallback. |
| Participant Advocate | Represents consent, accessibility, fatigue, privacy, and dissent; provides a confidential correction path. |
| Remote Science Coordinator | Advises on research priorities but cannot override field safety authority. |

The adviser has no travel, command, scientific, or safety authority.

## Seven-step cycle

### 1. Frame

Limit use to organizing already-approved information and comparing high-level research-plan options. Exclude autonomous navigation, vehicle or shelter control, personnel scoring, medical triage, biometric inference, survival guidance, and expansion of mission scope. State that staying put, turning back under the approved plan, and cancelling research are legitimate options.

### 2. Verify

Label the source, age, coverage, and uncertainty of every material input. A qualified person corroborates conditions relevant to movement or shelter. Gaps caused by remote communications or field instruments remain visible; the system must not present a prediction as a direct observation.

### 3. Compare

Compare the approved manual plan, a safe hold or deferral, cancellation, a reversible change in research sequence, and the AI-assisted option. Evaluate life-safety exposure, shelter and reserve capacity, crew workload, privacy, accessibility, repairability, communications loss, and the possibility that rescue is unavailable.

### 4. Authorize

The Expedition Leader and Field Safety Officer independently authorize consequential changes. The Medical Lead controls health decisions. Any participant may decline non-emergency research activity or request review without losing access to essentials. Remote science priorities never create field authority, and loss of contact never transfers authority to the AI.

### 5. Act

Provide a short advisory record with evidence, uncertainties, rejected alternatives, authorizers, and the approved fallback. Use the minimum data and least intrusive option. A human announces the decision, identifies how to raise a concern, and confirms that the non-AI plan remains usable.

### 6. Observe

Track near misses, overrides, plan reversals, communication gaps, resource and shelter margin, participant fatigue, privacy incidents, dissent, and whether one person or subgroup bears disproportionate risk. Do not convert safety monitoring into general behavioral surveillance.

### 7. Repair

Pause affected work, return to the approved fallback, correct the expedition log, notify responsible reviewers when communication permits, support affected participants, and examine related decisions. Remove the adviser if it increases uncertainty, coercion, workload, or unsafe reliance.

## Boundaries

- The adviser cannot choose a route, authorize travel, control equipment, or issue emergency instructions.
- Independent navigation, communications, safety, and mission-abort processes remain outside the AI workflow.
- Essential shelter, care, supplies, communication access, and a safe fallback cannot be conditioned on research participation or consent to expanded data use.
- Personal location and health information are minimized, access-controlled, and never used for performance or "fitness" ranking.
- A participant may question an output or request a pause without retaliation.
- The expedition does not infer consent from silence, hierarchy, isolation, or limited communication.
- Remote organizations may advise only within declared authority; field safety authority and escalation rules remain human and pre-established.

## Mandatory pause and stop triggers

**Pause consequential AI-assisted planning** when relevant inputs are missing or stale, required reviewers cannot participate, the team cannot show a viable non-AI fallback, communications loss blocks a review required by the mission plan, participants are too fatigued for meaningful authorization, or a participant reports a credible safety or privacy concern.

**Stop using the adviser and revert to the approved manual process** when it represents estimates as observations, proposes action outside scope, weakens reserve or shelter protection, pressures a participant to continue, exposes sensitive location or health data, repeatedly conflicts with qualified review, or becomes entangled with vehicle or safety controls. Any urgent response follows separately approved expedition procedures.

## Measures and counter-metrics

| Intended measure | Required counter-metric |
| --- | --- |
| Relevant information surfaced for review | Missed facts, false alarms, and uncertain coverage |
| Planning time saved | Added verification burden and rushed authorization |
| Reversible research-plan changes | Loss of shelter, reserve, or fallback margin |
| Manual overrides correctly recorded | Overrides discouraged, delayed, or ignored |
| Participant understanding of decisions | Perceived coercion, fatigue, accessibility barriers, and unvoiced dissent |
| Data minimized as designed | Location, health, or behavioral data exposed or repurposed |

Research output, schedule adherence, and distance covered are never standalone success measures.

## Non-substitution disclaimer

This case illustrates governance questions only. It is not polar travel advice and does not replace trained expedition leadership, meteorology, medicine, engineering, occupational safety, legal requirements, equipment documentation, rescue planning, or an approved emergency plan. It provides no real route, operating limit, frequency, repair method, or survival procedure.
