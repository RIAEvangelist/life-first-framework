# Worked Example: Crewed Mars Mission

This is a **fictional, non-operational** case study. The mission, spacecraft, habitat, crew, equipment, thresholds, and events are invented. It provides no real vehicle parameters, trajectory, vulnerability, communications details, repair instructions, or emergency procedure.

## Scenario

A crewed mission is traveling toward and later operating near Mars. Communication delay prevents conversational ground control, communication may be unavailable, repair and medical resources are constrained, and timely external rescue cannot be assumed. Independent life-support, navigation, communications, safe-state, and abort or fallback capabilities are governed by separately approved human mission rules.

An onboard AI adviser is proposed to organize approved system summaries and crew reports, identify uncertainty, and compare human-proposed maintenance, workload, and resource-conservation options. It cannot actuate equipment, navigate, allocate essential resources, diagnose or triage people, change work assignments, command the crew, or initiate, delay, or veto an abort or fallback.

## Why Life First applies

Delayed outside review and limited rescue or repair make local judgment essential and error recovery difficult. Long isolation may amplify fatigue, hierarchy, privacy concerns, interpersonal pressure, and automation bias. A resource optimization that appears efficient can endanger future fallback capacity or distribute hardship unfairly. The system must strengthen informed human deliberation without becoming an unelected mission authority.

## Risk tier

**Tier 3 — Critical.** Recommendations may influence life-sustaining conditions over a period when external correction is unavailable. AI remains advisory; qualified crew retain independent life-support, safe-state, and abort or fallback authority.

## Named roles

| Role | Authority and responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Mission Commander | Accountable onboard mission owner within approved mission rules and delegated emergency authority. |
| Vehicle and Habitat Systems Lead | Validates technical evidence and owns human-directed rollback and independent fallback readiness. |
| Crew Medical Officer | Controls clinical decisions, confidentiality, and medical resource priorities. |
| Crew Agency and Welfare Officer | Protects accessibility, fatigue, privacy, dissent, and non-retaliation; may request a safety pause. |
| Earth Mission Director | Exercises declared ground authority when communication and mission rules permit, recognizing delayed context. |
| Independent Mission Assurance Board | Reviews design evidence, exercises, incidents, and retirement decisions outside immediate mission pressure. |

The adviser is not crew, ground control, a clinician, or an authorizer, and delay never transfers command authority to it.

## Seven-step cycle

### 1. Frame

Limit purpose to organizing approved evidence and comparing options proposed by qualified humans. Exclude actuation, navigation, clinical decisions, personnel scoring, autonomous rationing, private-behavior analysis, emergency command, and abort or fallback control. Declare that life, dignity, crew agency, and future recovery capacity outrank schedule, scientific yield, and arrival targets.

### 2. Verify

Separate direct observations, crew reports, qualified interpretations, Earth-originated information, inferences, and unknowns. Record source age in both creation and receipt context so delayed guidance is not mistaken for a current order. Missing communication, degraded sensing, inaccessible components, and disputed crew reports stay visible.

### 3. Compare

Compare no change, the documented non-AI process, a safe deferral, a reversible precaution, use of a preapproved redundant capability, mission replanning, and the AI-assisted option. Evaluate present risk, repair inventory, crew workload and welfare, medical needs, communication delay, future life-support and abort capacity, and consequences of another failure before outside help.

### 4. Authorize

The Mission Commander and Vehicle and Habitat Systems Lead independently authorize consequential technical action. The Medical Officer controls clinical boundaries. Ground approval is obtained when mission rules require it and delay permits. Pre-established onboard authority governs during delay or outage; the AI cannot bridge ambiguity by acting on its own.

### 5. Act

Present a time-bounded advisory record containing sources, uncertainty, alternatives, authorizers, stop conditions, and fallback. Humans act only under approved mission and equipment rules. Maintain an independent means to isolate the adviser and continue safe operation without its history or availability.

### 6. Observe

Monitor safety and reserve margin, near misses, overrides, repair burden, crew fatigue, disagreement, privacy, workload distribution, delayed-ground conflicts, and automation bias. Invite confidential concern reporting and do not use wellness or communications data to infer loyalty, competence, or compliance.

### 7. Repair

Contain harm, withdraw affected output, restore the approved non-AI fallback, correct onboard and delayed-ground records, support affected crew, and examine similar decisions. The independent board reviews material events when communication permits. Retire the adviser when safe separation, correction, or trustworthy uncertainty reporting cannot be maintained.

## Boundaries

- AI is advisory only and isolated from life-support, navigation, propulsion, safe-state, communications, and abort or fallback controls.
- Independent life-support and human abort or fallback authority remain usable without the adviser.
- The adviser cannot make medical decisions, ration essentials, assign hazardous work, discipline crew, or resolve conflict in mission authority.
- Protected reserves and future recovery capacity cannot be consumed merely to preserve schedule, science, destination, or public narrative.
- Every crewmember may raise a concern, correct personal information, and decline non-emergency hazardous work without retaliation.
- Private health, interpersonal, voice, and off-duty data are not used for behavioral prediction or "mission suitability" scoring.
- Delayed Earth guidance is evaluated against current onboard evidence by authorized humans; neither old instructions nor AI inference silently override current human authority.

## Mandatory pause and stop triggers

**Pause consequential AI-assisted action** when input lineage or current applicability is uncertain, required onboard review is unavailable, delayed ground guidance materially conflicts with current crew evidence, fatigue impairs authorization, fallback readiness cannot be shown, mission authority is disputed, or any crewmember raises a credible safety or agency concern.

**Stop using the adviser and restore the independent non-AI workflow** if it influences actuation, claims emergency authority, hides delay or uncertainty, recommends bypassing mission controls, consumes protected life-support or abort capacity, pressures or profiles crew, exposes sensitive information, or repeatedly conflicts with qualified review. Immediate life-safety, safe-state, navigation, and abort responses follow separately approved mission procedures.

## Measures and counter-metrics

| Intended measure | Required counter-metric |
| --- | --- |
| Material evidence surfaced for crew review | Missed facts, false alarms, and unresolved contradictions |
| Decision preparation time reduced | Verification burden, fatigue, and rushed human authorization |
| Reversible actions supported | Failed rollback and erosion of independent fallback capacity |
| Resources conserved | Safety margin lost, care deferred, or hardship distributed unfairly |
| Delayed ground guidance reconciled | Authority confusion and stale guidance treated as current |
| Crew-reported clarity and usefulness | Automation bias, suppressed dissent, privacy concern, and perceived coercion |

Arrival, schedule, mission output, and resource efficiency cannot offset degraded life safety, crew agency, or future recovery capacity.
