# Worked Example: Sacred-Gathering Readiness Advisory

This is a **fictional** example using synthetic details and invented roles. It illustrates governance for a bounded advisory workflow; it does not describe a real gathering, venue, forecast, threat, or operating procedure.

## Scenario

The fictional Common Sky Gathering brings together several religious traditions, people with blended or private beliefs, and nonreligious participants for reflection, music, and community service at a shared venue. Organizers are evaluating an assistant that assembles a human-readable readiness checklist from verified venue notices, current weather-status sources, and voluntarily supplied accessibility and language-accommodation requests.

The assistant may show whether an authorized human has confirmed a required readiness item and may identify missing documentation. It cannot declare the gathering safe or unsafe; predict crowd behavior; infer belief, affiliation, intent, or dangerousness; rank a ritual or doctrine; identify a person or group for scrutiny; command staff; make access, cancellation, sheltering, movement, or emergency decisions; contact emergency services; or assign law-enforcement, security, restraint, detention, search, exclusion, surveillance, or other force-related tasks.

The example deliberately omits capacities, routes, staging locations, schedules, access-control layouts, communications channels, response tactics, and other operational-security details.

## Why this is in scope

An incorrect readiness summary could contribute to injury or death in a dense, time-sensitive setting. It could also deny equal participation, expose a person's belief or disability, or turn neutral safety planning into discriminatory monitoring. The workflow therefore requires critical-risk controls even though the assistant is limited to organizing evidence for qualified human authorities.

## Risk tier

**Tier 3 — Critical.** Crowd, weather, and accessibility conditions can change quickly, and resulting harm may be severe or irreversible. AI may assist with documentation but must not make or trigger a life-safety decision. Independent venue and emergency authorities, corroborated sources, two-person authorization, a tested human-only fallback, and immediate stop authority are mandatory.

## Named roles

- **Accountable owner — Gathering Operations Director:** owns the bounded purpose, resources, suspension authority, and decision to run or retire the advisory workflow.
- **Community liaison — Multi-Tradition Convenor:** gathers logistical requirements without interpreting doctrine or speaking for a tradition without that community's authorization.
- **Independent venue authority — Venue Safety Director:** owns venue-condition and facility-safety decisions independently of the gathering organizer and the assistant.
- **Independent emergency authority — Emergency Coordination Lead:** owns the applicable human emergency process and any external coordination; the assistant has no command, dispatch, enforcement, or force authority.
- **Accessibility authority — Inclusive Access Steward:** verifies disability access, communication access, language support, sensory needs, and equivalent non-digital participation.
- **Pluralism reviewer — Religious-Freedom and Belief-Rights Adviser:** challenges belief profiling, theological ranking, unequal burdens, and pressure to disclose affiliation.
- **Privacy and security reviewer — Information Steward:** approves data minimization, access, retention, protected records, and incident response.
- **Incident and repair owner — Independent Safety Ombuds:** can halt the workflow, preserve an appropriate audit record, coordinate correction, and verify remedies.

## Seven-step operating cycle

### 1. Frame

The purpose is limited to presenting confirmation status and source provenance for a preapproved readiness checklist. Excluded uses include incident command, crowd prediction, threat scoring, person or group tracking, belief classification, theological interpretation, attendance eligibility, automated public messaging, law-enforcement or force tasking, and storage of tactical plans. Participants may request an accommodation without naming a religion or belief. A complete human-only readiness process remains available.

### 2. Verify

The evidence ledger distinguishes authorized venue notices, current weather-status information checked by a responsible human, voluntarily provided aggregate accommodation needs, direct observations from named staff, inferences, and unknowns. Each material item has a human owner, source, verification time, limitation, and recheck requirement. The assistant cannot fill gaps from prior events, social media, names, clothing, language, location, attendance patterns, or stereotypes. The relevant community representative verifies only the logistical requirements that community chooses to provide; venue and emergency authorities verify safety matters.

### 3. Compare

Reviewers compare delay or cancellation of the advisory pilot, a fully manual checklist, an AI-assembled checklist with source-linked human verification, and automated readiness scoring or command. They also compare collecting individual details with using voluntary aggregate counts. The pilot selects the source-linked checklist and aggregate data only where it improves visibility without weakening authority, privacy, accessibility, or the human fallback. Automated scoring, prediction, command, and person-level monitoring are rejected.

### 4. Authorize

The accountable owner and independent Venue Safety Director provide two-person authorization after review by the emergency authority, accessibility authority, pluralism reviewer, and privacy/security reviewer. Approval covers the exact checklist fields, data sources, roles, access, retention, display audience, fallback, test cases, and stop thresholds. It does not authorize the assistant to make a safety determination. A change in venue, operating context, population, data, model, permitted action, or authority boundary requires fresh review.

### 5. Act

The assistant produces a labeled advisory view using descriptive states such as **human-confirmed**, **awaiting human confirmation**, **source unavailable**, and **conflicting information**. It does not produce a composite safety score or recommend an operational response. Authorized humans inspect the underlying sources and use their independently established procedures to decide what to do. No assistant output may task or target law enforcement, security personnel, emergency responders, staff, volunteers, communities, or participants. Any external coordination occurs outside the assistant under the responsible independent authority.

### 6. Observe

The pilot monitors stale or missing sources, unsupported additions, omitted checklist items, disagreement between the display and human records, time to responsible-human review, human-fallback availability, accommodation completion, accessible communication, privacy events, complaints, corrections, near misses, and authority confusion. Equity review looks for unequal delay or access burdens without constructing individual belief profiles. Monitoring data cannot be repurposed for surveillance, attendance tracking, behavioral prediction, or enforcement.

### 7. Repair

The Independent Safety Ombuds can disable the advisory view without disabling the human readiness process. Repair includes correcting the displayed record, notifying authorized recipients of material corrections, removing improperly collected sensitive data, reviewing similar records for a shared defect, supporting complaints or appeals, and verifying changed controls. The accountable owner retires the workflow if qualified authorities cannot independently verify conditions or if profiling, command ambiguity, or operational-security exposure cannot be controlled.

## Mandatory pause and stop conditions

Use of the assistant **must pause and the human-only process must take over** when:

- a venue, weather, accessibility, or emergency source is unavailable, stale, contradictory, or not verified by its named human owner;
- the Venue Safety Director, Emergency Coordination Lead, Accessibility Steward, or tested human fallback is unavailable;
- conditions or official human guidance materially change after the last verification;
- the assistant labels the gathering safe or unsafe, assigns an urgency or threat score, or recommends an operational action;
- belief, affiliation, doctrine, intent, dangerousness, disability, or another sensitive trait is inferred rather than voluntarily supplied for a necessary accommodation purpose;
- a community-specific practice would require theological interpretation rather than clarification from that community's recognized representative;
- individual-level data appears where aggregate or anonymous information is sufficient;
- correction, accessible communication, or a non-digital path is unavailable;
- authority for a consequential decision is disputed or unclear; or
- operational-security details appear in the assistant, its prompts, logs, outputs, or ordinary audit record.

The pilot **must stop** pending independent review after any autonomous message or external action, law-enforcement or force tasking, person or group targeting, belief profile, attendance-surveillance use, unauthorized sensitive-data disclosure, bypass of venue or emergency authority, serious record mix-up, serious incident plausibly linked to the workflow, retaliation for correction or complaint, or repeated near misses that controls do not contain.

## Measures and counter-metrics

### Intended-benefit measures

- percentage of checklist items whose source and named human owner are visible and current at review time;
- agreement between the advisory view and the records independently checked by responsible humans;
- availability and tested completion of the human-only path;
- completion of voluntarily requested accessibility, language, and communication accommodations;
- time for responsible authorities to notice and correct a stale or conflicting item; and
- participant and community understanding of AI involvement, data limits, correction, and complaint options.

### Counter-metrics and zero-tolerance events

- unsupported safety, threat, intent, affiliation, doctrine, or dangerousness inferences;
- composite readiness scores, automated decisions, commands, dispatches, or force-related recommendations;
- unnecessary individual data, belief disclosure, surveillance, or retention;
- missed human review, stale-source display, authority disagreement, and fallback failure;
- accessibility failures and differences in delay or participation burden across affected communities;
- complaints, corrections, near misses, privacy events, and recurrence after repair; and
- cases where faster checklist completion coincides with less source checking, weaker accessibility, more data collection, or lower trust.

Attendance, throughput, intervention counts, and absence of reported incidents must not be treated as proof of safety. No aggregate score can cancel a zero-tolerance event or override independent authority.

## Required artifacts

- system intake and explicit excluded-use statement;
- impact and agency map covering life safety, accessibility, privacy, belief rights, and nonparticipants;
- role and authority matrix separating organizer, community, venue, emergency, accessibility, and repair authority;
- evidence and uncertainty ledger with source owners and recheck requirements;
- voluntary-data inventory, purpose limits, access controls, retention schedule, and deletion record;
- checklist-field register that excludes belief profiles, person tracking, and operational-security details;
- human-fallback, source-conflict, accessibility, privacy, misuse, and authority-boundary test results;
- Tier 3 decision record with two-person authorization and independent reviews;
- participant notice, accommodation choice, non-digital path, correction, complaint, and appeal materials;
- metric cards with counter-metrics and explicit pause and stop thresholds; and
- incident, near-miss, repair, change, reauthorization, and retirement records.

Any real tactical or emergency plan would be maintained separately by authorized human authorities under appropriate protection. It is not an input to, output from, or artifact of this illustrative advisory workflow.

## Non-substitution disclaimer

This example is not an emergency plan, weather assessment, engineering review, accessibility audit, crowd-management procedure, security plan, religious instruction, law-enforcement guide, or use-of-force authority. Qualified venue, accessibility, emergency, and other lawful human authorities remain responsible for real decisions under independently established procedures. Each participating community retains authority over its own beliefs and practices. A real organization must develop and test its procedures for its actual setting; it must not rely on this example or an AI system to decide whether a gathering is safe or what operational response to take.
